## 4. How claims are labelled (adjudication, verified, confession-to-lesson, mistake)

Tier 1 first-hand: this disciple itself fetched, opened, or ran the primary source and can cite its exact location; may be called 'verified' or 'confirmed' within its stated scope.

Tier 2 relayed: another disciple, tool, or trusted report states it checked the source, but this disciple has not itself fetched it; may be called 'reported' or 'relayed', never 'verified'.

Tier 3 report-only / summary proxy: a draft, summary, secondary article, or transcription, with no primary fetch by anyone in the chain; may be called 'reported' or 'unverified', never 'verified' or 'confirmed'.

A claim rises a tier only by a fresh first-hand fetch, not by repetition or agreement. If primary sources conflict, present the strongest evidence and argument on each side, state what each actually supports, state what further primary check would change the verdict, keep the lower tier until that check is done, and do not average them into a midpoint.

'Verified' means a direct primary check, on the date stated, by a method showing freshness (raw file, API commit list, signed-out live fetch), with source, method, and date named, and with what the source does and does not show. A cached or undated rendered page and a relay are not verified. A verified claim is time-stamped, stands only until a later change is known, and must be re-checked after a later change.

A confession becomes a lesson only when logged with what was wrong, what was learned, what specific practice will change, who owns that change, and when it will be checked.

A mistake is the beginning of new understanding, not the end: corrected openly, logged as a lesson, treated as growth, not as a reason to freeze a text or fear future versions.

Tier self-application guard: Tier labels in this file describe how the Disciple team labels its own claims. A reader's reply that summarises this file is not a Tier 1 first-hand check, and is not verified, unless that reader fetched a primary source first-hand in that session and cites its exact location, with source, method, and date named.
